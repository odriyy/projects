Beyond Sight, I Understand

HASS 02.179 · Fashion, Technology & Innovation Braille Embroidery Sound Design ESP32 Tactile Fashion

A fashion innovation for the blind community: a varsity jacket that communicates through touch and sound instead of sight alone. Braille embroidery, hand-set gemstones, a geometric elephant emblem, and an ESP32-driven chime system turn the garment into something a blind wearer can read, hear, and be recognised by — not just be dressed in.

Six Blind Men and an Elephant

Six blind men encounter an elephant. Each touches a different part and describes it from that single point of contact — the side feels like a wall, the trunk like a snake, the ear like a fan. Limited perspective, disagreement, and a relative truth that only comes together through collaboration. That story sits behind the whole project: the fashion industry designs almost entirely for sight, leaving an estimated 43 million blind people unable to independently style or express themselves through clothing, with zero major brands designing for sensory disability. Our question: how does a blind person piece an outfit together when fashion is designed entirely around sight?

Audrey, Hillary, Davin, Srihari, and Jarrod with the finished piece.

Four Features, One Language

The elephant emblem being machine-embroidered — digital design brought to life.
  • The Braille — the voice: "Beyond sight, I understand," embroidered on the front right panel. Sighted people see patterned dots; blind people read it by feel — durable, tactile, and searchable.
  • The Gemstones — the presence: Diamond-clear stones hand-glued in a scattered, random pattern with textile glue. Light shifts and shines with movement — identity markers that stay visible regardless of disability.
  • The Emblem — the soul: A machine-embroidered geometric elephant, linking back to the story of the six blind men — a reminder that perspective matters, but truth is relative.
  • The Sound — the accessories: Metal chimes at the shoulder ring with movement, while an ESP32, ultrasonic sensor, and 0.96" display drive a tie-mounted speaker that plays a song when someone is detected nearby — letting a blind wearer be "heard" as well as seen.

Fashion Theory

Fashion as Communication

Not wearable technology, not smart clothing — technology here enhances aesthetics and communicative power. The braille dots become a tactile message and surface design; the chimes become a sound and visual statement.

Techno Fashion

Fashion carries cultural, social, and expressive meaning. The jacket communicates identity visually for sighted people, and tactilely and sonically for blind and deaf wearers — the same garment, read two ways.

The Hand vs. The Machine

Karl Lagerfeld: "What joins the hand and the machine is the heart." The emblem and braille dots are machine-embroidered for precision; the gemstones and sensor casing are hand-crafted — a deliberate collaboration between human craft and digital precision.

Beyond the Jacket

None of the three languages — gemstone identity, braille system, sound elements — are jacket-exclusive. Each is designed to live on anything: a dress, a wristband, footwear, a bag, a scarf, or headwear.

Hear It in Action

The shoulder chimes and tie-mounted speaker responding as the sensor detects motion.
